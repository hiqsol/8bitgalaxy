# Comparison review

Comparison of the current design with the most mechanically similar games.

## Closest games

1. **A Handful of Stars** is the closest overall:
	1. Space empire deck building on a shared map.
	2. Hand cards power colonization, technology, movement and control.
	3. Decks cycle and develop during play.
	4. Combat escalates by committing cards, resources and nearby fleets.
	5. Unlike this game, units are counters rather than cards and each turn has
	   two actions.
2. **Northgard: Uncharted Lands** is the closest non-space game:
	1. In-game deck building drives exploration, movement, recruitment and
	   construction on a modular map.
	2. Unlike this game, units are miniatures, cards only issue actions and
	   combat uses dice.
3. **Imperium: The Contention** is the closest combat comparison:
	1. Compact space 4X with a map of location cards, asymmetric factions and
	   assets deployed at locations.
	2. Repeated face-down commitments create bluffing.
	3. Unlike this game, decks are constructed before play rather than built
	   during play.
4. **Andromeda's Edge** is the closest match to the stated genre mixture:
	1. Space 4X, ship placement and engine building on a modular board.
	2. Unlike this game, it has no central deck-building loop and combat uses dice.
5. **Voidfall** is the closest low-luck 4X comparison:
	1. Scenario-based space 4X with asymmetric factions, technology, engine
	   development and deterministic combat.
	2. Unlike this game, cards select actions instead of becoming cycling actors
	   on the map.
6. **Dune: Imperium** is the closest deck-building and worker-placement comparison:
	1. Cards place workers, provide resources and can be committed to conflict.
	2. Unlike this game, it has little spatial movement, exploration or
	   persistent unit geography.
7. **Star Realms** is the closest turn and deck-cycle comparison:
	1. Draw-hand-discard cycle, hand as the turn budget, persistent bases and
	   direct attack.
	2. Unlike this game, it has no spatial field, movement, colonization or placement.
8. **Core Worlds** and **Eminent Domain** are secondary comparisons:
	1. Both combine space-empire development with deck building.
	2. Neither has a shared spatial map with direct unit movement and positional combat.

## Distinctive combination

1. The design is closest to **A Handful of Stars** map deck building combined with:
	1. **Andromeda's Edge** ship placement and engine building.
	2. **Star Realms** turn economy.
	3. **Imperium: The Contention** combat bluffing.
2. Its distinctive feature is that one [[Actor]] card can:
	1. Cycle through [[Reserve]], [[Hand]] and [[Discard]].
	2. [[Takeoff]] into a shared [[Field]] and become a spatial unit.
	3. Use all abilities as a direct participant or one ability as a [[Support]]er.
	4. Return to the deck cycle after [[Deplete]].
3. Other distinctive mechanisms are:
	1. Double-ended cards for [[Class]], [[Level]], [[Ready]] and [[Depleted]].
	2. The back side for [[Damaged]] and [[Hidden]].
	3. [[Slot]] pressure and overlap.
	4. Cards on the [[Field]] being stronger than the same cards in [[Hand]].
	5. Deterministic [[Combat]] with hidden commitments.

## Classification

1. **Card-driven spatial 4X with deck and engine building** describes the
   current rules more precisely than worker placement.
2. Conventional worker placement uses workers to claim or block action spaces.
3. Here, [[Slot]]s limit spatial presence while cards provide abilities to
   actions; they are not action spaces.
