# Comparison review

Comparison of the design with other games: the closest ones, what is
distinctive and what to take from others.

## Closest games

1. **A Handful of Stars** is the closest overall:
	1. Space empire deck building on a shared map.
	2. Hand cards power colonization, technology, movement and
	   control.
	3. Decks cycle and develop during play.
	4. Combat escalates by committing cards, resources and nearby
	   fleets.
	5. Unlike this game, units are counters rather than cards and each
	   turn has two actions.
2. **Northgard: Uncharted Lands** is the closest non-space game:
	1. In-game deck building drives exploration, movement, recruitment
	   and construction on a modular map.
	2. Unlike this game, units are miniatures, cards only issue
	   actions and combat uses dice.
3. **Imperium: The Contention** is the closest combat comparison:
	1. Compact space 4X with a map of location cards, asymmetric
	   factions and assets deployed at locations.
	2. Repeated face-down commitments create bluffing.
	3. Unlike this game, decks are constructed before play rather than
	   built during play.
4. **Andromeda's Edge** is the closest match to the stated genre
   mixture:
	1. Space 4X, ship placement and engine building on a modular
	   board.
	2. Unlike this game, it has no central deck-building loop and
	   combat uses dice.
5. **Voidfall** is the closest low-luck 4X comparison:
	1. Scenario-based space 4X with asymmetric factions, technology,
	   engine development and deterministic combat.
	2. Unlike this game, cards select actions instead of becoming
	   cycling actors on the map.
6. **Dune: Imperium** is the closest deck-building and
   worker-placement comparison:
	1. Cards place workers, provide resources and can be committed to
	   conflict.
	2. Unlike this game, it has little spatial movement, exploration
	   or persistent unit geography.
7. **Star Realms** is the closest turn and deck-cycle comparison:
	1. Draw-hand-discard cycle, hand as the turn budget, persistent
	   bases and direct attack.
	2. Unlike this game, it has no spatial field, movement,
	   colonization or placement.
8. **Core Worlds** and **Eminent Domain** are secondary comparisons:
	1. Both combine space-empire development with deck building.
	2. Neither has a shared spatial map with direct unit movement and
	   positional combat.

## Distinctive combination

1. The design is closest to **A Handful of Stars** map deck building
   combined with:
	1. **Andromeda's Edge** ship placement and engine building.
	2. **Star Realms** turn economy.
	3. **Imperium: The Contention** combat bluffing.
2. Its distinctive feature is that one [[Actor]] card can:
	1. Cycle through [[Reserve]], [[Hand]] and [[Discard]].
	2. [[Takeoff]] into a shared [[Field]] and become a spatial unit.
	3. Use all abilities as a direct participant or one ability as a
	   [[Support]]er.
	4. Return to the deck cycle after [[Deplete]].
3. Other distinctive mechanisms, worth keeping:
	1. Double-ended cards for [[Class]], [[Level]], [[Ready]] and
	   [[Depleted]], the back side for [[Damaged]] and [[Hidden]]:
	   no tokens needed.
	2. [[Slot]] pressure and overlap, instead of fleet supply and
	   upkeep.
	3. Cards on the [[Field]] being stronger than the same cards in
	   [[Hand]]; [[Takeoff]] also thins the deck, no separate scrap
	   rule.
	4. [[Hand]] as the turn budget, drawn at the end of the [[Turn]]
	   and spent on defense: Star Realms plus TI action cards.
	5. [[Research]] → [[Create]] → [[Upgrade]], the proven Through the
	   Ages chain.
	6. Deterministic [[Combat]] with hidden commitments, which works
	   in Voidfall, Kemet and Dune: Imperium.

## Classification

1. **Card-driven spatial 4X with deck and engine building** describes
   the current rules more precisely than worker placement.
2. Conventional worker placement uses workers to claim or block action
   spaces.
3. Here, [[Slot]]s limit spatial presence while cards provide
   abilities to actions; they are not action spaces.

## Lessons from other games

| Game | What to take | Where |
|------|--------------|-------|
| Through the Ages | Discover with science then build, upgrade pays the difference (already: [[Research]], [[Create]], [[Upgrade]]). Wonders. Players seed future events (already: [[FutureEvents]]). Game ends when the Age III deck runs out. Tactics reward a mixed army. | [[Twilight]], [[Hyperstructure]], [[Tactics]] |
| Civilization (2010) | Four victories: capture a capital, culture, economy, technology. Tech pyramid instead of printed prerequisites. | [[Goal]] |
| Civilization: A New Dawn | Focus row 1-5: a used card drops to slot 1 and recharges over time. Trade tokens boost a card (already: [[Boost]]). Unique wonders. | [[Restore]], [[Hyperstructure]] |
| Voidfall | 3 cycles, each ends with evaluation: scoring and upkeep; Focus cards come back at the cycle end. Diceless combat, defender wins ties, max 2 fleets per sector (already: [[Combat]], [[Slot]]). Trade tokens unlock an extra action. | [[Backlog]] ideas |
| Imperium: Classics | Reshuffle is the progress tick: gain or develop a card. History pile: cards out of the deck still score. Game ends when a supply runs out. | [[Twilight]], [[Legacy]] |
| Twilight Imperium | Ships can't pass systems with enemy ships. Planets exhaust and ready every round. Home system has a space dock. Mecatol Rex is a prize in the center. | [[Pinning]], [[Restore]], [[Backlog]] ideas |
| Eclipse | Pinning. Sector tier is printed on the tile back (already: [[Space]] Length). Ancients guard the good hexes. | [[Ancient]], [[Pinning]] |
| Star Realms | Authority is the clock. Outposts must be destroyed first. One cost curve for every faction, thinning toward the top, a single card of cost 8 each (already: [[Deck]] Levels and crowns). | [[Guard]], [[Deck]] |
| Mage Knight | Deck building, hex exploration and diceless combat in one game. Wounds go to the deck and clog the hand: not taken, a Damaged card from a pile goes to the Field. | [[Academy]] |
| Tyrants of the Underdark | Deck building with area control. Promoting a card takes it out of the deck for points. | [[Legacy]] |
| Kemet | Diceless battle cards. Points for holding temples are lost with the temples. | [[Goal]] |
