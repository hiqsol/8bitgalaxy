# Warpwake

Variant 1 of 3, the faithful one: it keeps today's [[Lore]] page
(Warp, the Ancients, two young civilizations) and makes it exact. See
[[LoreVariants]] for the comparison.

Story: [[Homefall]], in Ukrainian, a comeback after the loss of the
homeworld.

## Name

**WARPWAKE**. Every warp bubble leaves a wake that the whole galaxy
can see, and the wake wakes the Ancients. The title carries both
meanings of the word, and those two meanings are the premise.

## Pitch

You have just opened your first warp bubble, and everyone heard it.
Somewhere across the dark a second young people opened theirs in the
same century, and they fly your ships. Something much older heard you
both.

## Tone

Hard SF, documentary. The canon is dry: manuals, charters, registry
entries, field reports. The stories open each chapter with a quote
from the canon. The quote states a law and the scene shows it at
work.

## Premise

1. **Warp** is a bubble of folded metric. It opens and closes only in
   the gravity well of a star. It carries ships, signals and the
   energy of the fold, which is huge but not limitless.
2. **Folds are huge**: a warp hull is kilometers of folded metric.
   A star's well holds only a few folds before space-time tears, so
   a whole star system has room for only a few ships and stations.
   Every working fold tears a little more.
3. **The Wake**: every bubble rings through the whole galaxy. A fleet
   can't move unseen. The only exception is a muffled bubble
   (Stealth), which has to stay small and slow.
4. **Marks and trims**: a design resonates at a Mark. Each design
   has two trims, the full trim (the strong End) and the reduced trim
   (the weak End), and a refit turns a design from one to the other.
5. **The Ancients** are an old civilization, far ahead of anyone
   else. Their guardians sit in the wild stars, unseen until they
   rise. They want the newcomers gone, and they hear every wake.
6. **Leaders** are living beings upgraded with warp. Each carries a
   fold of its own, so one leader is as potent as a great ship: it
   takes an orbit, reaches and acts like one. An Academy opens a
   child's first fold, and the leader grows from there.
7. **The Deep** is warp transit. A people keeps a few vessels or
   leaders in the Deep at a time. From there they can surface at any
   star where the people has presence, or lend one thing to an action
   anywhere. Nobody controls the order in which vessels come out of
   the Long Transit.

## Laws

Each law answers a required item of [[LoreRequirements]] by its
number. Facts that follow from it get a phrase in the same law. The
rule page stays the source of truth.

| # | Law |
|---|---|
| 1 | Ships are huge. A warp hull is kilometers of folded metric, and every fold tears the well it rests in. A well holds only a few folds before space-time gives way, and a vessel that finds no free orbit can't close its bubble there. Work widens the tear, so a vessel that works withdraws into the Drift and a station that works drops to reduced trim. Leaving is itself a fold, and no fold is free. A fold in a lower orbit shears the one above it. |
| 2 | A bubble opens and closes only in a star's well. Between stars there is nowhere to stop: you arrive or you never left. Every well you pass through costs the time it takes to fold out of it. |
| 3 | The Wake: every fold rings through the whole galaxy, so everyone sees everything. Only a muffling technology hides a fold, and a muffled bubble is small: it reaches one step and carries one load. |
| 4 | Every lane rings with its own echo. From home you hear its length; what lies in it you learn by going. |
| 5 | Bubbles merge, so a fleet reaches farther than any ship in it. A ship reaches as far as its Mark, and a station doesn't fold at all. Warships in a well lock passing bubbles there. A remote order travels as signal, and signal isn't locked. |
| 6 | A station doesn't hold a star against a ship: presence that can act outranks presence that only stands. Equal presences make a front: nobody holds it, and each side keeps its own there and gives orders from it. |
| 7 | Whoever holds the star holds everything that stands or lies wrecked in it. Every station there serves the holder, whoever built it, and wrecked leaders are its captives until they stand again. |
| 8 | People are raised at an Academy and machines are produced at a yard. Nobody is born great: the Academy raises the living only from the first Mark. A megastructure is both a people and a machine, so it is raised and built. What is destroyed can be built again. A people is measured by its living works. |
| 9 | A people with no living leader dissolves. A leader is a living being upgraded with warp, as potent as a great ship. The dead are counted, not the living: the wrecked, the unbuilt and the not yet understood. |
| 10 | A people is not its home. It lives while some star still takes its vessels and its orders, even a front. It is lost only when no star is left that it could surface into. |
| 11 | The guardians of a veiled star rise against those who come to take its worlds, more of them for a better world. What you defeat is a projection: it takes no wound and rises again elsewhere. Whoever takes a veiled star finds the world it is equipped for. |
| 12 | A people has four branches and four ends. Conquest ends a rival at once. Culture lives everywhere, production raises seven megastructures, and science holds every branch at its highest Mark at once, held and not spent. Each of those three ends begins the Last Cycle, in which every other people has one cycle to answer. |

## Human and Alien traits

Optional facts of today's Deck. Warpwake mentions them as traits of
the two peoples and builds nothing on them:

1. **Alike**: both peoples build the same designs, only the paint and
   the hands differ. Nothing forces it: a people builds what it
   wants, and later peoples will build their own.
2. **The Deep holds five**, and crews retune three stations a cycle.
3. **Marks 1 to 8**: as high as both peoples have reached.
4. **Ballast**: young fleets carry junk devices, the Tools.

## Canon

The in-world documents. Together they state every law:

1. **The Warp Primer**, a cadet textbook: stars, lanes, the Flat,
   reach, orbits.
2. **The Fleet Manual**: giving an order (starter, source and
   destination), groups, support, surveys, the cost of failure.
3. **The Charter of the Council**: the cycle, the Deep, the Muster,
   leaders, the four ends of a people, the Last Cycle.
4. **The Registry**: one entry per design and technology a people
   has built, with Mark, trims and branch. It stays open: a new
   design is a new entry.
5. **The Xenoarchaeology Reports**: Ancients, veiled stars, worlds,
   and later the events of the galaxy.

## Story seeds

1. **The Muffled Ship**: a single Stealth ship crosses the cluster one
   star at a time, because a hidden card moves with Range 1. Its
   whole crew knows that a merged bubble would carry them faster and
   would also be heard.
2. **Our School, Their Children**: the enemy holds a captured
   Academy and raises its own leaders in our classrooms.
3. **The Wrecked Leader**: a wrecked Hero lies in an enemy star, and
   a wrecked card belongs to whoever holds the star. Variant
   [[Assay]] tells this one, and its plot works here unchanged.
4. **Seven Wonders**: a Production people needs one more
   megastructure. A megastructure has to be raised and built in a
   single cycle, so the attempt has to fit into one Muster.
5. **The Last Front**: a people's only star is a front. The people
   lives there: it surfaces and gives its orders under the enemy's
   guns, while the enemy gives orders from the same star. Whoever
   strikes first may take it.
6. **The First Fold**: a child at the Academy gets its first fold
   and learns why nobody is born great.

## Strains

1. **The Deep**: the Hand is form, but the Deep gives it an image.
   Keep the image and skip the mechanics.
2. **Projections**: the Ancients need the Reports to say why they
   take no Slot and come back.
