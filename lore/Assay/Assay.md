# Assay

Variant 2 of 3, which reinvents the premise. The Ancients are not
exterminators. They are examiners, and the game is the examination.
See [[LoreVariants]] for the comparison.

Story: [[Habeas]], a captured hero.

## Name

**ASSAY**. An assay tests a metal for purity, and in the old sense
the word also means an attempt. In this variant the Assay is the
trial that admits a young people to the galaxy, so the trial and the
game share one name.

## Pitch

The galaxy is old, and it is full. No new people enters it unexamined.
When two young peoples reach the stars in the same age, the Elders
seal them into a small cluster of stars. They give each one the same
fifty-six warrants and watch what each makes of them. One of them
will be admitted. The Admissions Roll doesn't say what happens to the
other.

## Tone

Kafka with starships: courtly, ironic and exact. The Code of the
Assay is scripture, and the characters quote its articles the way
lawyers do. The Assayers rarely speak. When they do, their rulings
are final and slightly too polite. The stories are legal puzzles in
the manner of Asimov: a crisis, failed readings of the Code, then the
right one.

## Premise

1. **The Assay** is held in a sealed cluster, the Assay ground. Walls
   close it (the Void), and the Assayers own the dark between its
   stars.
2. **Warrants are real**: they are the cards. Nothing exists in the
   Assay without a warrant, whether a vessel, a colony, a station or a
   leader. A warrant has two readings, one at each End. Turning it end
   over end changes the reading.
3. **The Allotment**: each candidate receives its warrants, and
   nothing outside its Allotment can exist for it. In the Assay of
   Human and Alien both Allotments are the same, so the trial is fair
   the way white and black are fair.
4. **The Deal**: the Assayers deal each Council its warrants every
   cycle, one per seat of the Council, in an order nobody chooses.
5. **The Assayers** are the Ancients. They guard the unclaimed posts
   and examine every claimant, they are never wounded, and they
   decide the Reply.
6. **The Record is open**: every warrant exercised is entered in the
   public record. Only a Stealth warrant may be exercised face down.
7. **The berths**: the Assayers built the berths in every post, and
   that is the only place a warrant can be exercised. A post has only
   a few of them, for vessels and for works. The reason is physical:
   the berths hold vessels so large that a star's well can cradle only
   a few before space-time tears. The Code writes the limit down so
   that nobody tries to exceed it.

## Laws

Each law answers a required item of [[LoreRequirements]] by its
number. Facts that follow from it get a phrase in the same law. The
rule page stays the source of truth.

| # | Law |
|---|---|
| 1 | A warrant is exercised only in a berth, and a star's well can cradle only a few great hulls before space-time tears. An exercised warrant gives way: a vessel lapses to the Rest, and a work is endorsed "reduced". A lapse is a movement, and it is never free. A warrant in a lower berth covers the endorsement of the one above. |
| 2 | The dark between the posts belongs to the Assayers. A warrant may cross it but may not rest in it. |
| 3 | The Assay is public: every warrant exercised enters the open Record. The Code assumes the least of any warrant it cannot read, and only a Stealth warrant may be exercised face down. |
| 4 | The Assayers post the length of every passage, never its nature. |
| 5 | Warrants acting together reach as far as their sum. A vessel reaches as far as its rank, and a work doesn't reach at all. Where warriors stand, moving warrants must halt. A remedy, an endorsement or an issue is not moving. |
| 6 | A post is held by the candidacy whose warrants there can act; works alone don't hold a post against vessels. Where standing is equal, the post is contested: nobody holds it, and each candidacy keeps its own warrants there and issues orders from it. |
| 7 | The holder of a post holds every work and every fallen warrant in it. A leader is held by its own candidacy, unless fallen. |
| 8 | Living warrants are issued at an Academy, machine warrants at a Yard, and a Great Work at both. Every leader begins as a child: a living warrant is issued only at the lowest rank. A destroyed warrant returns to the Stores. In the final reckoning only living warrants are counted. |
| 9 | A candidacy lives while it has a living leader. A leader warrant is a living being upgraded with warp, so it is posted and ranked like a vessel. The Assayers count only the fallen, the unissued and the sealed. |
| 10 | A candidacy is not its home. It fails when no post is left where it could exercise a warrant. |
| 11 | The Assayers guard the unclaimed posts and examine every claimant, with more examiners for a better world. An examiner is never wounded and returns to the pool. A claimant is awarded the world it is qualified for. |
| 12 | There are four marks of admission. Conquest ends the Assay at once. Diaspora, the Seven Works and Singularity each call for the Reply, a single sitting for every other candidacy. The Assayers count what a candidacy holds, not what it has spent. |

## Human and Alien traits

Optional facts of today's Deck. In the Assay they are terms of the
Allotment:

1. **The Allotment**: both candidates receive the same fifty-six
   warrants, identical except for the seal.
2. **Five seats** on a Council, and three restorations a sitting.
3. **Ranks 1 to 8**.
4. **Petty warrants**: the Tools.

## Canon

1. **The Code of the Assay**: numbered articles, the laws above.
2. **The Schedule of the Allotment**: the fifty-six warrants, the
   [[Deck]].
3. **The Rulings**: case law. Each ruling settles an edge case the
   Code left open, like the referee's rulings of the playtests.
4. **The Minutes of the Council**: one candidacy's sittings in its
   own words.
5. **The Admissions Roll**: the record of past Assays: who was
   admitted, and a blank column for the others.

## Story seeds

1. **The Petty Warrant**: a junk Tool turns out to be the one warrant
   that completes a Prerequisite pair.
2. **The Child**: the last leader has fallen, and the Council has one
   sitting to issue a child warrant from the Stores. The child is
   buried under a warrant that no Academy can issue.
3. **The Reply**: one sitting to answer a claimed mark.
4. **The Blank Column**: a clerk tries to learn what happened to the
   candidates who were not admitted.
5. **Seven Works**: two Great Works must be issued in halves within a
   single sitting.

## Strains

1. **Fiat**: the Code explains every rule by the Assayers' choice.
   That fits the tone, but it doesn't test the rules: an arbitrary
   rule still reads well here.
2. **A game inside the game**: the world is literally a trial, and
   some readers may feel the stakes are staged. The Admissions Roll's
   blank column is there to raise them.
3. **Exterminators or judges**: today's [[Lore]] says the Ancients
   want the newcomers gone. Here they admit one candidate and say
   nothing of the other.
4. **Fixed warrants**: the Allotment limits what a candidacy can
   build, while a lore sets no limit on designs, see
   [[LoreRequirements]]. The Allotment would have to become a
   starting grant, not a ceiling.
